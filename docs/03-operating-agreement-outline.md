# Operating Agreement Outline

Education only — not legal advice. An operating agreement is your LLC's internal rulebook: who owns what, how decisions are made, and what happens if a member leaves. Most states don't require you to file it, but you should keep it with your business records. Single-member LLCs benefit from one too — it helps show the LLC is a separate entity from you personally.

*Asdale Dynamics, LLC is an Indiana LLC. Indiana does not require an operating agreement, but Indiana law (IC 23-18) defaults to statutory rules when no agreement exists — a written agreement overrides those defaults.*

## Indiana-specific notes

- Indiana does not require filing an operating agreement with the state
- Keep it at your principal office or with your business records
- If you don't have one, Indiana's default LLC act (IC 23-18) governs — including default rules on voting, distributions, and dissolution that may not match what you want

## Disclaimer

This outline is educational only and is not a substitute for an attorney-reviewed operating agreement. Multi-member LLCs, LLCs raising money, or LLCs in regulated fields should have a lawyer review the agreement.
